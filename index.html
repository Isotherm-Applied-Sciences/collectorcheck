About CollectorCheck

CollectorCheck stores paper and digital receipts privately in one place. It lets people sort, manage, categorize, and calculate their expenses without complexity.

The CollectorCheck.com site is not a business. CollectorCheck is not monetized. There are no subscriptions, no upsells, and no paid tiers.

We’re building it publicly on GitHub because we don’t have an investor base — we have a community. The repo is open because the problem it solves is bigger than any one person or company. We need contributors, testers, and people who understand the real‑world chaos of modern work.

Receipt chaos is real.

Digital receipts didn’t simplify anything — they scattered everything. Receipts now hide in inboxes, apps, spam folders, platform dashboards, login‑locked archives, and proprietary formats.

People juggling multiple income streams, contract work, remote work, gig work, or small business activity are dealing with the same mess. The documentation burden has quietly shifted onto individuals, and most people don’t realize it until tax season.

Governments have already responded.

Across North America and Europe, documentation rules have tightened: expanded reporting requirements, stricter self‑employment audits, more digital‑only receipts, more platform‑specific payout records, and more emphasis on accurate expense tracking. If you earn income in non‑traditional ways, you need continuity.

Gig workers feel this the most — but they’re not alone.

Gig work participation keeps rising: 16–18% of U.S. workers, 10–12% in Canada, 11–14% across the EU, and 20–25% monthly participation in major cities.

Gig workers generate more receipts than any other group — often 20–40 per week, 1,000–2,000 per year. Fuel, food, parking, maintenance, tolls, supplies, subscriptions — it adds up fast. Freelancers, contractors, remote workers, and small business owners face the same documentation overload.

This isn’t just a gig‑worker problem. It’s a modern‑work problem.

Why CollectorCheck exists.

The shift from “side hustle” to real income created a new kind of paperwork chaos. More people are working in more ways: multi‑platform income, hybrid employment, contract work, remote work, small business activity, gig work, and part‑time stacking. But the tools for managing receipts and continuity never evolved with the work itself. People are left with scattered documentation, lost deductions, and unnecessary stress.

CollectorCheck Receipts is a small, free, open‑source tool designed to bring order to the chaos. It’s being built publicly on GitHub because the need is real, the problem is growing, and the people who understand it best are the ones living it.

CollectorCheck is early‑stage but active. The repo is open. Development is ongoing. And we’re looking for participants who want to help shape it.
